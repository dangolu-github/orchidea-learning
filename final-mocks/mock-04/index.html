Reading Task 1 — Match headings

Choose the best heading A–L for paragraphs R01–R10. Two headings are extra.

How a Small Museum Reached New Visitors

R01 The Harbourside Museum had few visitors under thirty. Staff assumed younger people disliked local history. A survey proved otherwise: many had never heard of the museum, although they walked past it.

R02 The museum asked eight students to explore the collection for an afternoon. Staff did not give a tour. Instead, students photographed surprising objects and recorded the questions they raised.

R03 One student chose a cinema ticket and wondered who had bought it. Another found a sailor's damaged cup. Their questions started sixty-second audio stories written with the museum historian.

R04 Professional actors were unaffordable, so residents recorded the stories. A local radio producer used the museum's quiet room and microphone to show volunteers how to speak naturally.

R05 Visitors scan codes beside objects. Paper transcripts are available, and the museum lends audio players to anyone without a phone. Staff wanted to improve access, not create a barrier.

R06 The first recordings were tested before the public launch. Listeners liked the voices but could not always find the correct object. Designers responded by adding a small colour photograph beside every code.

R07 The museum did not advertise with expensive posters. Students shared one sample story on community social-media pages. Within days, it was passed between thousands of local accounts.

R08 Visitor numbers rose, but staff wanted better evidence. A voluntary exit card asked how people had heard about the museum and which story they remembered. This showed that the sailor's cup was the most popular.

R09 Not every response was positive. Some visitors wanted more dates and facts. The museum therefore added a “learn more” label linking each story to a detailed information sheet rather than making the audio longer.

R10 The next series will focus on objects donated by recent immigrant families. The museum will invite donors to decide what can be shared publicly. Staff say this permission process matters as much as recording quality.

Reading Task 2 — Multiple choice

Read the article and choose the best answer for R11–R20.

One Week Without Recommendations

For one week, journalist Caleb Wong decided not to accept any automatic recommendation from a screen. He turned off suggested videos, ignored “customers also bought” lists and chose music by searching for exact names. His aim was not to prove that recommendation systems were harmful. He wanted to notice how often they influenced ordinary decisions.

The first day felt slow. Instead of opening a suggested news story, Caleb visited three publications and compared their front pages. He read fewer articles but remembered more of them. Choosing evening entertainment was harder. He and his flatmate spent twenty minutes discussing films, then discovered that talking about what they wanted was more enjoyable than silently scrolling.

By Wednesday, Caleb noticed a disadvantage. Recommendations sometimes help people find creators they would never search for. Without them, his music choices became safe and familiar. He solved this by asking colleagues for one album each. Their suggestions were less exact than a computer's, but each came with a personal reason and started a conversation.

Shopping produced a different result. Caleb had expected to spend less when extra products were not placed in front of him. This happened, but only slightly. The bigger change was that he delayed two purchases because searching required effort. A day later, he realised he did not need either item.

At the end of the week, Caleb restored some recommendations. He kept them off on shopping sites but allowed them for music. He also changed one habit: when a recommended article makes him feel strongly, he now waits before sharing it and checks the original source. His experiment did not produce a simple rule. Instead, it showed that convenience can be useful, provided it does not replace attention.
