Reading Task 2 — Short answers and meaning

Read the article. Answer R11–R20 in no more than five words and/or a number.

The Library of Things

When people move into a small flat, they often buy objects that are used only once or twice a year: a drill, a carpet cleaner or a large cake tin. The Eastgate Library of Things offers another choice. Members borrow useful household objects instead of owning them.

The project began with 120 donated items stored in an unused room at the ordinary public library. Its name sometimes causes confusion: it is not a separate building, and it does not lend books. Today, the collection contains more than 600 objects. The most frequently borrowed item is a sewing machine, followed by a pressure washer.

Membership costs £18 a year. Most loans then cost between £1 and £6, depending on an item's value and maintenance needs. Members can reserve online, but they must collect and return objects in person so staff can check their condition. A late return leads to a daily charge because another member may be waiting.

Before borrowing electrical equipment, members watch a short safety video and answer five questions. Staff demonstrate more complicated objects, such as carpet cleaners. These rules have kept damage low: fewer than three percent of loans require a repair. When something does break, the team first checks whether a local technician can fix it.

The library measures more than money saved. It estimates that members avoided buying 1,900 new products last year. However, project manager Aisha Grant says the figure is only an estimate because the library cannot know whether every borrower would really have bought the object. Aisha's next goal is to create collection points in two neighbourhood shops, making the service easier to reach without adding another large building.
